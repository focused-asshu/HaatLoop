# Product hypothesis — unvalidated

Small vegetable vendors may have edible produce left near closing time. Nearby buyers may be willing to reserve it at a vendor-set price and collect it locally. HaatLoop proposes connecting these groups. These are hypotheses, not findings; no research has been conducted.

## Users and focus

The proposed initial users are small vegetable vendors and nearby buyers in Odisha, India. Language, connectivity, device access, accessibility needs, market locations, and trading routines require founder research. No market size or adoption claim is established.

## Proposed MVP journey

A vendor lists edible surplus with quantity, unit, price, and pickup window. A buyer finds an appropriate listing, reserves a quantity, then collects it. Payment happens directly between buyer and vendor. The system records the reservation outcome without claiming to verify external payment.

## Scope

Future MVP scope: vendor-controlled listings, nearby discovery, stock-safe reservations, pickup coordination, and recorded outcomes. Phase 0 implements none of these; it provides tooling and a foundation screen only.

Out of scope: platform payments, delivery, AI/speech, realtime features, fake dashboards, fabricated transactions/reviews/testimonials, and impact claims. Auth, geolocation, marketplace flows, and backend integration are deferred beyond Phase 0.

## Risks and assumptions to validate

- Vendors may prefer WhatsApp or existing informal channels.
- Incremental revenue is unproven; a discounted sale may displace an existing sale.
- Buyer pickup economics are unproven; time and travel cost may outweigh savings.
- Vendors may lack time or reliable connectivity to maintain accurate listings.
- Stock can change at the stall while buyers reserve online.
- Food safety, reporting, pickup deadlines, cancellations, and no-shows need operational policies.
- External payments cannot be inferred from a completed pickup record.
- Local language and trust requirements remain unknown.

Do not present this hypothesis as customer evidence. Founder research and manual experiments should precede claims or expanded product scope.
